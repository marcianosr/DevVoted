# Changelog
All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## Unreleased
### Added
- **YAGNI pays you for the room you are not using.** A new 1-slot config. Your build rents space in fixed sizes — 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 — and pays for the size, not for what it actually holds, so a 9-weight build rents 12 and is charged for three slots standing empty. YAGNI takes **8 KB a gate off the rent for every empty slot**: that 9-weight build now pays 40 instead of 64. Worth most to a build sitting low in a wide rung, worth nothing on the free four, and **it can cost you** — it fills a slot itself, so installing it into a build already flush with its rung tips you into the next size up and the bill goes up instead of down. While you hold it, every install asks twice and states the new bill first, because inside your current rung an install now costs you too. Unlocks by reaching gate 4 having never paid upkeep.
- **`&&` chains your correct answers into storage that doubles.** A new 4-slot config. Every correct answer adds a link to the chain and pays storage for it: 1 KB for the first, then 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128 and 256 — nine right in a row takes the whole 320 KB a run can earn from answers, and the config sits quiet after that. A wrong answer short-circuits the chain straight back to 1 KB; a partial neither extends it nor breaks it. Unlike the streak on the coverage meter, **the chain does not reset when you clear a gate** — it carries across gates, and across days, until you get one wrong. Its row on the poll screen always states what the next link pays before you answer, so you know exactly what a miss costs you. Unlocks after closing five perfect windows.
- **The climb map is back, and it says more than it used to.** The community board draws the whole ladder again: thirteen gates in a row, each in its own colour, joined by a dashed track that scrolls sideways on a narrow screen and opens centred on the gate you are standing on. Your gate is filled in; everything past the deepest point you or your best run ever reached stays dimmed, so the mountain still has an unknown top. Under each gate stand the people who are there — their photo or initials, wearing the border they bought — with the crowd folding behind a **+4** where a gate is busy, and the runs today's gates killed sitting greyed out in a lane of their own beneath them. A star marks the deepest gate you have ever finished on. Three things the old map never said are now on the chips themselves: a **rim** in the gate's colour means that climber's last gate closed PERFECT, a slow **flicker** means it closed SHAKY, and a small **tag** means their run was resumed from a git tag rather than started at the bottom. Anyone you traded an audit with today — whether you fired at them or they fired at you — wears an orange ring, so the incidents listed under the map have faces on the map. **Tap any avatar** and their build opens under the track: every config they are running, at the version they are running it, with the one they vendor-locked badged, or "nothing installed" if they are climbing bare. Your own chip is ringed green, matching the way the board already marks your seat everywhere else.
- **Tap a climber and their card opens.** A foldout on a phone, a panel under the track on a wider screen. It names them, links them to their GitHub account, and shows the title they wear. Under that: where they stand, how their last gate closed, the share of coverage they have banked, the weight their build carries against the space it rents, and what is left in storage. Their whole build is there as config chips, at the versions they run, with the one they vendor-locked badged. Three tiles carry their current streak, the category they have answered right most often, and the gate they are on. The card ends by saying what it will never show you: answers, unanswered polls and prefetch stay private. **What another player may see about your run has changed**: your storage, the coverage you have banked and your streak used to be yours alone and are now part of your standing, on the same footing as the build everyone could already read. What a run knows that you do not — your picks, your peeks, your estimates, the band you promised, whether you have a shot held — is still private, and always will be.
- **The old game is remembered.** DevVoted has been rebuilt, and the daily-poll game that came before it does not carry over: those runs cannot be resumed, replayed or re-scored under the new rules. What does carry over is the fact that you were there. Every account that started a run back then is granted **Legacy Tester**, a title no account made after the rebuild can ever hold. If your run was still open on the day the rebuild landed — you were mid-climb and the ground moved — you are granted **Legacy Climber** as well, and that one is rarer by definition, because the moment has passed and nobody else can be caught by it. Both are yours permanently, both show up wherever your worn title shows, and neither can be bought, earned or taken away. Nobody outside that group ever sees them offered or listed, so they are not a bar anyone can chase. The first time you open the game you are told once what you were given and which of your old runs went into the archive, and you can put one on from there — after that the notice is gone and the titles live on your profile like any other.
- **The storage you banked survives the rebuild, and the old guard get a top-up.** Your archive is the storage a run leaves behind when it ends, and it carries across the rebuild untouched: whatever you had banked is still there, at the number you left it. On top of that, every account that started a run in the old daily-poll game is credited **256 KB**, which is exactly what the cheapest profile border costs, so it is something you can go and spend rather than a number that nudges a bar. If your run was still open on the day the rebuild landed, you are credited **1 MB** instead. It lands once, on the accounts that were already there, and nothing you do afterwards can earn it.
- **Titles: earned identity you wear beside your name.** Your account has said what you own — a border you bought, a role somebody assigned you — and nothing about what you have done. **Titles** are the other thing. Each one is a bar on your own record, never a comparison with anybody else: **25 correct Git answers** makes you **Git Maintainer**, a correct answer in every one of the twelve categories makes you **Completer**, twenty perfect windows makes you **Flawless**, and taking a run all the way through the thirteenth gate makes you **Summit**. They cannot be bought, they cannot be taken off you, and nobody else holding one stops you earning the same. That last part is the whole difference between a title and a **category leader's seat**, which is a standing somebody can beat tonight and which stays exactly where it was. Once you cross the bar the title is yours permanently — the check is never run a second time, so a record you later fall short of never costs you the title you earned with it. You can hold as many as you earn and **wear exactly one**, chosen on your profile, where the ones you have not earned are listed too with the bar each of them wants. What you wear shows on your profile, under the byline of any poll you wrote, and on your build when a rival opens it up to decide whether to fire at you — compact avatars on the climb stay as they are. Titles settle when a run ends, and any title you have not been shown yet is announced the next time you open the game. Three of them work differently on purpose: **First Ascent** goes to the first account that ever clears all thirteen gates and can never be won again by anyone, and two are granted rather than earned — see below. An account **role** — Poll editor, Admin — is unchanged and stays on the handle line where it always was: it says what somebody is allowed to do, which is a different claim from what they have done, so the two now sit on separate lines and never share a word.
- **The Dex learns the registry's services.** The collection screen gains a **services** tab: one list of everything the shop and the archive sell beside configs, eight in all. Each row says where the service is bought and how long it lasts (`Registry · this visit`, `Run · carries into your next run`, `Next run · consumed on start`) and what it costs. A service you have not earned yet is named all the same, with a ? for its glyph and one line saying how to earn it where the price would go, in the shop and in the Dex alike. Rebuild is yours from the start; Extend unlocks the first time you reach Cascade, the git tag the first time you reach gate 4, Hot Reload after five rebuilds, Return Policy after five sells, kill -9 once you clear gate 5, Boot Cache the first time a run banks 256 KB, and Docker Image the first time you finish a run still holding a config you were dealt. Once earned a service is yours in every run after, and the shop still waits for its gate before selling it. The four whose press is not built yet start counting today and read *not for sale yet* until it is.
- **kill -9 ends a run from the shop.** Abandoning is a service now: earned by clearing gate 5, then in every shop, free. It takes two presses, the first arming the row and the second ending the run on the spot. Nothing banks, and the next run starts fresh the same day.
- **Builds are open.** Every other player's build is now something you can read. On prep, each rival offered to your attack lists what they have installed under their name — every config with its version and weight, and the one they vendor-locked — so you know what you are firing at before you fire. On the community board, press or hover any climber on the climb, the fallen included, to read theirs. What you cannot see is anything that would hand you an answer: their current poll, their picks, their peeks, their estimates or the band they promised. A build is public; a run's answers are not.
- **Every category has a leader, and the community board seats all twelve.** Under the byline on every question there is now one line naming who leads that poll's subject: the longest unbroken run of correct answers anyone has ever strung together in it, whose it is, and the figure itself — "Git · leader · @giovanni · 13 in a row". Hold it yourself and your avatar is ringed and the figure goes green. The community board states the same thing at full size, in place of standouts: **twelve rows, one per subject**, held seats at the top by their run, with the scope on the panel head ("longest run of correct answers · all-time") and a count of how many are taken ("9 of 12 seated"). A run breaks on a wrong answer and only on a wrong answer — a part-right select-all holds your place without advancing it, exactly the way the streak bonus has always behaved — and it counts every answer you have ever given in that subject, across every run and every loop the game has had. A subject nobody has managed **3 in a row** in shows **unranked · 3 in a row claims it**, which is the one place the game tells you the number outright. The figure is a **best**, not a live streak, so nobody loses a seat by missing: a seat changes hands when somebody beats it, and the board says so under the rows. Answers given under the Mirror audit are left out, since those answered a different question, and a gate that hides which subject you are being asked about hides the line with it.
- **Audits are now fired at you by other players.** No gate deals an audit on its own any more: gates 3 to 7 have room for one, 8 to 10 for two, Elite and the Champion for three, and a gate nobody attacked is clean. Clear a gate HEALTHY and you hold one attack; clear it PERFECT and you get to choose between two rolled payloads. On prep, a new **Your attack** panel offers three rivals at your gate or ahead who last cleared strong — never anyone struggling, never the same player twice running — and one press fires. You do not pick the audit: it is drawn from their next gate's pool, and it lands when they clear the gate they are in, so it is on their stake receipt, with your name on it, before they walk in. Surviving a rival's audit pays **+32 KB** per audit on the clear, itemised on the debrief; nobody earns anything from your death. Every attack filed today is public on the new **Incidents** page, reachable from prep and the community board, with your own rows ringed. An attack lasts your whole run until you fire it. Two things this replaces: 402 is no longer the guaranteed first audit at Thunder, and the Champion no longer always deals 408, 410 and 413.
- **A poll now tells you how the room did on it, and what you did to it last time.** Two lines sit above every question. The first says how hard other players actually found it — "brutal · 31% got it right first time" — counting each player once, on the very first time the poll was dealt to them, before any reveal had taught them the answer. That is the only version of the number that keeps meaning the same thing as a poll gets older. The bands run `brutal`, `hard`, `fair`, `easy`; a poll fewer than five people have tried says `untested` and states no figure rather than calling a question brutal on the strength of four wrong answers. The second line only shows up if you have met this poll before: "answered twice · you missed it both times, last on 4 Aug". It counts answers, not deals — a poll you linted away or let the clock take has no result to report. Answers given under the Mirror audit count toward neither, because those answered a different question. The option count and answer type have moved down onto the first line, and still describe whatever the poll is *presenting*, so 207 Multi-Status keeps its secret.
- **New audit: 207 Multi-Status, where every poll looks like a select-all.** Gates have always told you whether a poll wants one answer or several, above the options and again in the shape of the keycaps. At a 207 gate they stop saying. Every poll arrives with square caps and reads *multiple answers*, whether it really takes one or five. Nothing about the answers themselves changes: a poll that wants one answer still wants exactly that one, so a second pick **cancels** the right one instead of costing you nothing, and a genuine select-all still pays its partial rungs. What you lose is the premium. A select-all normally pays double, and at a 207 gate everything pays the same as a pick-one — you cannot be rewarded for aiming at a difficulty you were never shown. Two things get sharper: **.length** tells you how many correct answers the whole gate holds, so a count of 5 across 5 polls proves every one of them is a single, and **Prefetch** and **git rebase -i v2** keep naming the types outright. 207 never shares a gate with 300 Multiple Choices, 404 Not Found or 451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons — they all attack the same reading step, and stacking two of them is not a gate, it is a wall. It can turn up from gate 4. Afterwards, the reveal and the review tell you what the poll really was.
- **New config: Regression Test, which pays you for the questions you have got wrong.** Every config on the roster so far pays according to what you built or what the gate is doing. **Regression Test** (2 weight, 64 KB) is the first one that pays according to *your own record*: a poll you have answered before without getting it fully right is worth **×2 coverage** the next time it is dealt to you. A part-right select-all counts as a miss, because a regression test covers a case that did not pass. The set is read fresh every time your run loads, never stored, which is what makes it honest: answer one of those polls correctly and its test retires, so the config is worth **less** the better you get. That decay is the whole balance, and it is the one config in the game you can price out of your own build by learning. It reads on any category, since a miss is not a subject. It unlocks by **answering 25 polls correctly** — you need a record before it has anything to read.
- **New config: `strict: true`, a wager you arm one poll at a time.** Every config you own has been something you install and then forget about: it multiplies, it adds, it pays out, and it does the same thing on every question whether you know the answer or not. **`strict: true`** (1 weight, 32 KB) is the first one that asks you something. Before you answer, you can arm it. Get the answer **exactly** right and it pays **half a unit** on top of whatever the rest of your build earned you; anything less than exactly right, a partial select-all, a miss, or running out of time under a timed audit, and it takes **half a unit straight off the gate's tally**. That last part is new to the game: until now a wrong answer cost you the slot and nothing more, and the coverage bar could only ever go up. An armed wager is the one thing that can move it back down. It cannot take a gate below zero, so the worst an arm can do is cancel what that gate had earned, and it **disarms itself after every answer**, so there is no such thing as leaving it on and forgetting: each poll is a separate decision about how sure you are. The press sits on the config's chip in your build bar and reads **arm ±0.5**, lighting up as **armed ±0.5** once it is live, and the poll header states **wrong costs 0.5** for as long as the wager is up, so the risk is on screen before you commit rather than in the receipt afterwards. When it pays, it shows up as its own line on the answer's receipt and the chip flashes like anything else that contributed; when it loses, the receipt bills it as **wager lost** and takes it off the total. A partial counts as a loss, which is the whole point of the name: a compiler flag that rejects "nearly" cannot pay out on "nearly". It is worth most on single-answer polls in a category you know cold, and it is a genuinely bad idea on a select-all you are guessing at. It unlocks by **answering 50 polls correctly**, late enough that you have some idea how often you are right.
- **A finished run keeps its own page.** Every climb that ends now has a lasting address of its own, so the run-over report is something you can come back to rather than something that vanishes when you start the next one. It shows only your own runs.
- **New config: vendor lock-in, which buys you room by taking away your exit.** Build space is rented by the gate now, and the rung you hold bills you whether or not you have filled it, so the heaviest config in your build is usually the one deciding what you pay. **vendor lock-in** (4 weight, 128 KB) names one config in your build as your vendor: its weight stops counting against the space you rent, so the room it frees is a rung you no longer have to pay for. A 12-weight build carrying an 8-weight config can drop to the 8 rung and cut its bill from 64 KB a gate to 32. What it costs is not storage. That config can never be sold or dropped again for the rest of the run. It asks which config the moment you install it — in your opening build or in the shop — and the screen will not let you leave until you say, so the weight is never spent on nobody. It cannot name itself, and it is worth taking only on a config heavier than its own 4 weight, which is the decision it exists to pose: the thing worth exempting is the thing you would least like to be stuck with. A missed gate can still peel the config you locked, because a peel is not a sale, and selling vendor lock-in itself releases the lock and hands you the weight back. It unlocks by locking 3 configs in.
- **New config: Math.ceil(), which rounds your ragged partials up to whole numbers.** A select-all poll pays double, and a part-right one pays a quarter, a half or three quarters of that — which leaves two figures on the ladder that are not whole: catch a quarter of the key and you are paid 0.5, catch three quarters and you are paid 1.5. **Math.ceil()** (2 slots, 64 KB) rounds both of them up. A quarter caught pays **1**, three quarters pays **2**, and a half catch is left alone because one unit was already whole — the config says nothing on the polls where it did nothing, so it never takes credit it did not earn. It cannot fire on a single-answer poll at all, where an answer is either right or not, and its chip says so before you commit: **select-all only** on a single, **pays on a partial** on a select-all. The top-up is flat, like Code Coverage's, so no multiplier amplifies it. That is deliberate and it is what keeps the ladder honest: on a bare build a near-miss and a perfect select-all both pay 2, but put anything that multiplies coverage beside it and the full answer pulls ahead again. It unlocks by **being paid for 10 partial answers**, which is the behaviour it exists to reward. Worth pairing with a gate running **300 Multiple Choices**, which turns every poll in the window into a select-all.
- **The end of a run is its own screen now, and it reports the whole climb.** A finished run used to hand you the *last gate's* debrief with a "the run ends here" box bolted underneath, which meant the one moment you wanted to look back at ten gates of answers showed you five of them. **Run over** opens on where you stopped, how many of the thirteen gates you held, and the swatches you are keeping. Under that: **coverage**, the bar as it closed and how far short of its line it landed; **gate by gate**, one row per gate with what each of its five polls paid, the gate that paid most flagged **best**, totalling to the score the bar is drawing; **by category**, every right answer you gave across the run sorted strongest to weakest, with your **best** subject and your **leak** named; **the build at the end**, the configs you were holding, what the space you rented billed you each gate, and what upkeep cost you across the whole climb; **storage**, split into what banks into the archive and what burns with the run; and **unlocked**, the swatches that stay on your profile, the configs you registered for future runs, and — stated plainly rather than left for you to discover — the build and the run balance that do not carry forward. A summited run gets the same screen with the Champion's colour instead of red and **The climb is done** at the top, because a win and a loss deserve the same accounting. It leaves on **Start new run**, with the community board beside it rather than instead of it.
- **Every answer now shows you where its score came from.** A right answer used to pay a number and say nothing about it: a correct JavaScript question paid **1.3** whether or not you understood that the `.js` config in your build was the reason. Every score in the gate's payout strip now opens its own receipt: hover a figure and it lists what made it, one line per thing that contributed, closing on the figure you were paid. Each line states the **units** it added, so the lines add up to the total in front of you — a config that multiplies shows the factor it was sold at as a tag beside its name and the units it actually produced as its figure, so `×1.25` on a right answer reads `+0.25`. Because it lives on the chip rather than under the question, it explains every poll in the gate, not only the one you just answered. At the same time, **every config in your build says what it is worth on the poll in front of you** rather than leaving you to guess: `×1.25 here` when it is paying, `idle this poll` or `JavaScript or TypeScript only` when it is not. And when the answer lands, **the configs that actually paid flash**, so the cause and the number arrive together. If your build is folded away at the bottom of the screen, the folded bar flashes instead, so you never miss it.
- **New config: Dry Run, which shows you what an answer is about to do to the gate**: the coverage meter has always told you where you stand and what the gate wants, and left the arithmetic between those two numbers to you. **Dry Run** (2 slots, 64 KB) does it for you, live, while your answer is still on the table. Pick an option and the meter grows a second mark: a red line at the level a **wrong** answer would leave you on, beside the dashed block that already showed what a right one pays. The two together are the reading you actually want at gate 9 with 40% held and 60% needed, which is not "how much is this poll worth" but "can I afford to be wrong here". Sometimes the answer is yes, and the red line sits above the demand, meaning this poll cannot cost you the gate however it goes. Sometimes it sits at zero, because coverage never goes negative and you were already close enough to the floor that a miss takes everything you built this gate. Worth being clear about what it does **not** do: it tells you nothing about the poll in front of you. Both marks are built from the same per-answer figures the screen already quotes, so they are identical whichever option you have selected, and toggling between answers moves neither one. Dry Run sells you the consequence, never the answer, and a config that could price your specific picks would be an answer key wearing a meter.
- **Configs are earned now, and the Dex is the checklist that fills in**: every account starts with eight configs (`.js`, `.ts`, `.css`, ESLint, Unit Tests, Code Coverage, IndexedDB, Cold Start); the other 27 each unlock **individually**, and the Dex's Configs tab shows exactly how. A locked config is a `???` silhouette carrying its two paths as plain text with live progress: a thematic objective that teaches the config's own mechanic ("Peek the community split 5 times · 3/5" unlocks Telemetry, "Sell 3 configs in a single shop" unlocks WTFPL), or a lifetime polls-answered fallback ("Answer 100 polls · 43/100"), whichever lands first. Nothing is activated and nothing can be missed: every answer, peek, sell, lock, estimate, reorder and clear counts by itself, in abandoned runs too, and a mirrored gate's flipped answers still count toward their real category, because they still prove you knew it. The moment a target is crossed, the grant announces itself where it happened: a saffron **unlocked** line on the reveal, the poll or the shop, a badged row in the gate clear's "What changed", and an "Unlocked this run" section on the game-over screen, each naming how it was earned. A granted row in the Dex keeps that provenance forever ("Earned: cleared Marsh's Mirror audit without a miss"), the starter eight read as what they are ("Starter config"), and the tab's count finally means something: granted of 35, not a total congratulating itself. The shop shelf is untouched, so a config you have not earned can still be bought and used mid-run, and using it is often exactly how its objective is met. For now the starting hand still deals from the free eight; dealing from your own collection arrives separately, so the unlocks you bank today are the pool you will draw from then.
- **Enter submits your answer**: the poll screen already let you press a letter to pick an answer, then made you reach for the mouse to send it. Pressing **Enter** now submits, so a poll can be answered without leaving the keyboard. Enter does nothing until something is picked, which is the same rule the Submit button follows, and the tip under the answers says so only while it will actually work.
- **A new config pays you for knowing your own accuracy**: **Planning Poker** is a one-slot config that asks a single question before a gate starts — how many of its five polls will you answer correctly? Commit a number from one to five, and if the gate ends on exactly that number it pays 32KB for every poll you estimated, up to 160KB. Anything else pays nothing at all, so the bet is the whole config. Two things make it more than a bonus for playing well. It pays out on a **failed** gate exactly as readily as a cleared one, because calling your own bad day is the same skill as calling a good one, and it is the one config on the roster that a miss does not silence. And because the payout grows with the number you call, the tempting bet always sits just above the honest one — which is the joke, since estimating is the thing developers are famously worst at. The estimate is made on the prep screen and **locks the moment you answer the first poll**, and while you are answering, the config's own row keeps score against it ("estimated 4 · 2 right so far"). Get it wrong and the gate report finally has something to mark as failed: no config could ever fail a step before, because none of them had made a claim.
- **New config: git rebase -i — decide which poll you meet first**: the order a gate deals its five polls has always been the daily seed's business, which quietly decided a lot for you. **Cold Start** doubles the gate's *first* answer, **Overclock** quadruples it and then runs hot on everything after, **Cache** pays more the longer you stay right in one category, and **Dependabot** counts correct answers in a row — every one of them cares where a poll sits, and none of them let you do anything about it. **git rebase -i** (4 slots, 128 KB) does. On the prep screen it lists the gate's five polls **by category**, each next to the coverage you already hold there, and moves any of them up or down the queue. Put React first and Cold Start fires ×2 on the category you are best at; push Java to the back and Overclock's ×0.5 tail lands on the poll you were going to miss anyway. **The order locks the moment you answer** — this is a bet placed before you read a single question, and knowing the category is not knowing the question. It reveals **only** the categories: option counts, which polls take more than one answer, and the next gate's mix all still belong to **Prefetch**, which stays the better buy if what you want is to see. The two do not need each other, and rebase never reorders anything but your own run — the seed everyone climbs is untouched, so a posted result stays comparable. Worth knowing before you buy it: on its own it changes no number at all. It is only worth slots next to a config that cares about position, and in a build with none of them it is four slots of sightseeing.
- **New gate rule: 451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons**: a gate can now arrive with its answers redacted. On a gate carrying 451, the window's **first three polls** each show two of their answers as `?????` instead of text, and **4 KB buys one back** — the same price every time, however many you unseal. A sealed answer is still pickable, so guessing blind is a real play rather than a dead end: you might recognise the question well enough to gamble, or spend 8 KB to be sure. Two answers on every poll always stay readable, so no poll is ever a coin flip, and which ones are sealed has nothing whatsoever to do with which one is right — `?????` never points at the answer. The linter refuses to cross out a sealed answer, since telling you a hidden option is wrong would be a cheaper way of reading it than paying; buy it back first and the linter will touch it. 451 never turns up alongside 300 Multiple Choices or 404 Not Found, which already make a poll hard to read. It can share a gate with 403 Forbidden, which freezes the linter and the peek — but never the buy-back, because a sealed answer you are forbidden to read would be a trap rather than a rule. Like every gate rule it is named on the stake receipt before you walk in, and everyone climbing that day meets the same one. Whatever you gambled on, the answers are all named in full on the reveal afterwards.
- **New config: Garbage Collection — a miss can pay you back**: failing a gate makes you free slots by dropping configs or minifying them, and until now that removal returned nothing at all: the config left and took its price with it. **Garbage Collection** (2 slots, 64 KB) turns the peel into a sale. Every config you **drop** to settle the quota refunds exactly what selling it in the shop would have, so shedding a 256 KB config hands back 128 KB toward rebuilding, and the repair screen prices each row next to its remove button so you can see which config is worth the most on the way out. **Minifying pays nothing** — the config is still installed, so there is nothing to collect — which turns the two ways of paying a peel into an actual choice instead of a formality: keep half the effect, or take the money. It is priced by the same shelf the shop sells from, so WTFPL still voids every refund and Freemium still halves them, and it pays for its own removal if it is the config you decide to let go. It cannot save you from the miss that ends a run: when a peel is bigger than your whole build the run is over before the repair screen opens, and nothing is refunded. Worth knowing before you buy it: in a climb where you never miss a gate, it does nothing at all.
- **New config: yarn.lock — the shop's padlock is bought now**: locking an offer used to be a built-in control that arrived at gate 2 and held exactly one offer. It now ships with **yarn.lock** (1 slot, 32 KB): while it is in your build, every offer on the shelf carries the padlock — 16 KB locks one, rebuilds skip it, and every later shop leads with it until you install it or press **release**, which is free and refunds nothing. Lock as many as you like; each pinned offer occupies one of the shelf's slots, so freezing your whole shop is possible and entirely your own doing. The pins belong to the lockfile: sell yarn.lock, or lose it to a gate peel, and every lock releases with it. WTFPL still retires the padlock, since the whole roster is already on the shelf. And the Gates dex no longer promises "lock" at gate 1 — no gate hands it over anymore.

- **New config: SLA, where you say how well the gate will go before you answer it.** Every gate you clear pays the same, whether you scraped over the line or filled the bar — the band you close in decides whether you *survive*, never what you are *paid*. **SLA** (2 weight, 64 KB) is the first config that changes that, and it makes you say it out loud first. In prep you promise a band: **OK**, **HEALTHY** or **PERFECT**. Close in that band or better and the gate's own payout rises by **10%**, **25%** or **50%**. The catch is that the rate is read off the band you *promised*, not the one you reached: promise PERFECT and finish on HEALTHY and the agreement pays you nothing at all, where a modest promise of OK would have paid. Promise low and you are nearly always paid a little; promise high and you are often paid nothing, occasionally paid a lot. Beating your own promise still pays — it is a floor, not a bullseye, the same way Planning Poker's number is — and missing one costs you nothing beyond the uplift you did not collect, so a cautious promise is never a trap. It pays on the gate's own prize rather than on your interest, your faucet or anything else your build earns, so what it multiplies is exactly the figure the prep table already prints beside each band. It unlocks by **closing 10 perfect windows**, which is the thing it asks you to have the nerve to promise.
- **New config: Try/Catch, the one thing that can take back a run-ending gate.** Closing a gate in **DANGER** has been the only unconditional ending in the game: no retry, no peel, no choice. **Try/Catch** (4 weight, 128 KB) is the single exception. A gate that would have ended your run **holds** instead, exactly as if it had closed SHAKY — and the catch is spent doing it and deletes itself on the spot. The name is the whole mechanic: a catch handles the error in flight, it does not rewind it. You still owe the peel, you still have to run those five polls again, and the gate still shut on you. What you keep is the run. Its own 4 weight is the **first thing that peel takes**, so it settles four slots of the debt on the way out, and where your peel was four slots or fewer it settles the whole thing and sends you straight on to the shop. A gate it caught can never then kill you by peeling your build to nothing. The debrief says plainly what happened, and once it is gone your next DANGER close is fatal again — though nothing stops you buying another one in a later shop, at full price. At 4 weight it is not something an opening build can carry: it is insurance you buy once the climb is worth insuring. It unlocks by **clearing 40 gates**.
- **Three new audits a rival can fire at you: 425, 500 and 510.** **425 Too Early** sits your whole build out of a gate's opening poll — no coverage, no storage, no linter, no peek — while that poll's ordinary base credit still scores, which guts an opener build like Overclock and leaves it paying the throttle that bought the opener. **500 Internal Server Error** takes the coverage meter, the band beside it and the gate's score row away for the window's first four answers and hands them back for the fifth; nothing about scoring changes, you simply answer four polls without knowing where you stand, and your receipt still itemises what each one paid. **510 Not Extended** runs every upgraded config at v1 for the whole attempt: nothing goes offline, the versions you bought just stop counting, and a build you never upgraded shrugs it off. 425 can be drawn from gate 8, 510 only at Elite and the Champion, and 500 from gate 3 on.

### Changed
- **The Dex and your profile are one page now, and every player has a card.** The Dex was your collection with your name missing; the profile was your wallet with your collection missing, and visiting somebody else's landed on a bare row of digits. Both are now **one page per player**, opening with a card that shows the border you wear, your avatar, your name and your titles. `/dex` takes you there. Your own page carries the whole Dex under the card, plus two new tabs — **borders** and **titles** — so the things you wear are bought, worn and displayed in the same place; the border shop is no longer a hidden link in the menu. Somebody else's page gives you their card and four counts: polls seen, configs held, gates cleared, archive. Nothing behind the tabs, because your collection is your own record of what the game has shown you.
- **You can wear three titles at once.** Titles were held by the handful and worn one at a time. Your card now shows every title you wear, up to three, in the order you put them on. Everywhere else — the byline of a poll you wrote, your open build when a rival inspects it — still shows the first one, so a build row stays a build row.
- **Pressing a player's face takes you to their page.** Every name in the game used to lead off to GitHub and nowhere else. The **face** beside a poll's byline, a category leader's seat, a climber on the map and a rival in the attack panel is now a link to that player's page. The `@handle` next to it still goes to GitHub — one is who they are here, the other is who they are there.
- **409 and 426 name a config's version, not its level.** The two audits that aim at what you upgraded most and least still said "highest-level" and "lowest-level" while every config card reads `v2`. They now say version, so one word covers the upgrade everywhere.
- **Install is the brightest press on a card, and Uninstall tells you what you get back.** The press that spends your storage used to be the same dark tinted box as every other control on the card, so a shelf of offers read as a wall of identical buttons. **Install** is a solid pale button now — the one press a card is asking for, and the only thing on it wearing that weight. Uninstall went the other way: it lost the red **×** that made removing a config look like destroying it, and says the word with the storage it hands back beside it, in green, with a small return arrow: **Uninstall ↩ +32 KB**. That figure is the one thing you were weighing when you opened the card, so it is on the press rather than in a line of small print underneath — and the card's footer no longer repeats it.
- **The poll you are about to answer is marked on the gate's score track.** The row of five slots under your score showed the polls you had already answered and the ones still to come, but nothing said which of the empty ones was next. The poll in hand now stands in a lit dashed box, the same mark the map already uses for the gate you are standing on, so the track reads as where you are rather than only what you have banked.
- **The storage balance leads with the number, and tells you when it moves.** Your balance used to sit in the corner as a small badge the same size and weight as everything else around it, and it changed in silence: you would look up and the figure was simply different. It is a large figure now, with the floppy mark and the word **storage** tucked underneath it, so the one number that runs your whole run reads as the number it is. When it moves, it **counts** to its new reading rather than jumping — green climbing, red falling — and a small pill rises beside it naming exactly what landed: **+32 KB** for a payout, **−32 KB** for an install. The pill fades on its own after a moment. Changes that land close together **queue and play in order** rather than the last one wiping out the ones before it: install two configs in quick succession and you see **−32 KB**, then **−48 KB**, with the figure resting on the balance each one left before moving on. While changes are still playing the shelf holds back its **after install** line, so the two readings can never disagree. On the shop shelf, pointing at an offer you can afford — with a mouse or by tabbing to its Install press — now states **after install → 378 KB** under the balance, so the price on the button reads as what it leaves you with rather than a number you have to subtract in your head. Players who have asked their device for less motion get the new reading with none of the movement.
- **Categories are named in full, and they stand out in a sentence.** ESLint used to say it worked on "JS/TS polls" — a shorthand nothing else in the game uses, and which read as one lump rather than the two categories it actually covers. Wherever a category is named now, it is spelled the way the rest of the game spells it and wears a badge, so "Cross out a wrong answer on **JavaScript** / **TypeScript** polls" tells you at a glance which polls the config is waiting for. The badge takes no colour of its own, so a category stays a name rather than reading as a payout or a penalty. A config sitting a poll out says it is waiting for **JavaScript** or **TypeScript** too, and the upgrade panel and the dex now state the same sentence the same way — previously each read it differently depending on which one you had open.
- **Config cards lay themselves out by the room they have, and every panel folds at once.** The shelf and your build used to force two cards to a row whatever the screen was doing, which on a half-width column left a card too narrow to print the name of the config it was selling you: a chevron, a weight block, an Install press, and a nameless gap where the config should have been. Cards now flow as many to a row as actually fit and never below the width a card's header needs, so a wide panel fills across and a narrow one stacks. Each panel's header carries a small two-way chevron that opens or shuts every card in it at once, for when you want the whole shelf arguing at once or the whole build quiet. Three smaller things came with it: the weight block is wider and reads as a block rather than a boxed digit, the fold chevron lost its button outline and its highlight so the header reads as a row rather than a toolbar, and a config on the new-run shelf that you have already taken now says **Installed** instead of quietly losing its press. A card that no screen has given a fold to — the configs you pick from when a gate asks you to drop one — states itself outright instead of hiding its refund behind a chevron that does nothing.
- **A config's state on this poll is a badge again, and a draining cap counts itself down.** A config sitting the poll out used to say so in muted grey prose under its name — "TypeScript only", "pays at the clear" — which read as a card with nothing to report rather than one telling you why it is quiet. That reason wears a badge now, beside the figures the working configs show. And the configs that fill your storage from a shared run cap, **IndexedDB** and **Database**, carry what is left of that cap on their face: **320 KB left** at the start of a run, ticking down as correct answers draw on it, with the digits rolling rather than jumping. It stays on the card while the card is collapsed, which is where it matters — the cap is the thing that decides whether the config pays at all, and running dry is not something you should have to open a card to discover.
- **Your build says how much room is left, once, in the unit the header counts in.** The free space under your configs used to be one dashed box per empty slot, each reading "empty slot", and the new-run screen drew none at all. It is one dashed row now: **empty** on the left, and the weight you have left in a block on the right, as long as the room it stands for.
- **Every config says what it does, and the card opens where you are deciding.** A config used to be a single line — its name, its weight, a badge — with everything you needed to judge it hidden behind a small **i**: what it does, what it weighs, what it sells back for. That was a price you could miss, and on a phone the panel it opened was sometimes unreachable altogether. A config is a card now: a chevron and its weight on the left of the header, its name beside them, the press that acts on it on the right; then the effect in plain words, and a footer carrying its version on one side and **uninstalls for 16 KB** on the other. It reads the same everywhere a config is drawn — the shop shelf, your build, prep, the poll footer, the gate debrief and the run-over report. **Which cards start open depends on what you are doing with them.** A config you are deciding whether to buy arrives expanded and argues for itself; a config you already installed sits collapsed, so a full build stays a list you can scan rather than a page you scroll. The chevron flips any card either way, and what you flip is remembered per panel — a shelf that re-rolls or a build that gains a config does not lose the cards you opened. One consequence worth stating: a config you **cannot** sell — on a finished run's report, or another player's build — no longer quotes a sell price it would be nonsense to act on.
- **Planning Poker and SLA now hold the gate until you have called it.** Both configs ask you for something on the prep screen — a bet on how many of the five you will get right, or a promise of the band you will close in — and both used to let you walk straight past without answering. That was never a real choice: neither one can cost you anything. Falling short of your bet pays nothing, missing your promised band pays nothing extra, and in both cases that is the whole of it — no coverage comes off, no storage is billed. So the lowest call was always free money and skipping was always strictly worse, which made forgetting the only way either config could hurt you: a slot paid for, carried up the mountain, quietly paying nothing. Now the gate will not open until you have answered. **Start** stays on screen and says which config is still waiting and what it wants — "Planning Poker has no bet — call one above" — with the picker directly above it, so the thing that unblocks you is never on another screen. If two are waiting, both are named. Nothing about the payouts changed, and the call still clears at the end of every gate, so each window is its own fresh decision. The same press now also tells you when **vendor lock-in** is waiting to be pointed at something, which it never did before: prep was the one screen that went quiet and left you pressing a dead button.
- **The coverage a build quotes per right answer is the number the next answer pays.** Prep and the shop used to quote one fixed figure per build that left every opener out and folded every throttle in, so a Cold Start build read ×1.5 before its dead first answer and an Overclock build read ×0.5 before its ×4 one. The quote now follows the poll in front of you: the opener before the window's first answer, the throttle after it, and a Focus bonus only once the poll's subject is known.
- **Configs take up only the room they need.** In the shop and on the new-run screen, every config — the ones already in your build and the ones on offer — used to be stretched to the full width of its column, so a one-word name like **.ts** took up as much of the screen as **Build Artifacts** and five offers read like a form to fill in. They now sit at their natural width and flow across the column, the way the build already reads during a poll and the way the Dex lists them. Nothing about the offers themselves changes: the same configs, the same **Install** press with its price still spelled out on it, the same panel behind the **i**.
- **The answer's send belongs to the poll now.** The **Lock in** press used to ride the build bar, outside the poll's own border and under a heading that said Build. It now sits inside the poll, between the question and the byline that credits it, and the two bars stack: the build sheet holds the bottom of the screen and the press rides directly on top of it, always within reach of the question it commits. Open the build and the press lifts with it rather than disappearing behind it. Scroll to the end of a poll and each settles into place — the press above the byline, the sheet closing the page.
- **The poll and the coverage meter stand side by side.** On a wide screen the coverage meter moves out of the stack and into a column beside the question, where it follows the answers down the page instead of scrolling away above them — the question is now the first thing on the screen rather than the second. On a phone the question still comes first, with the meter under it. The name of whoever holds a category's seat is also written the same way as the poll's author now, and links to their GitHub account.
- **The collection screen is called the Dex.** Its heading read **Dex Registry**, two words only because plain "Registry" was already the shop's offer list. The navigation has always said Dex, and now the page agrees: **Dex — everything the game has shown you**. Registry from here on means one thing only, the list of configs the shop deals you inside a run.
- **The upgrade panel closes itself, and stops quoting the whole climb.** It ended on a line totalling what every remaining version would cost together ("all the way to v5 costs 448 KB"), which priced a purchase nobody makes in one press. That line is gone; the panel prices the one version it is offering. In its place, the panel carries a **×** beside the config's name, so it can be shut from itself rather than only from the chip that opened it.
- **The Dex's Configs tab reads as rows of chips, grouped by weight.** Each weight gets a row, heaviest first, headed with how much of it you hold ("2 weight · 4 of 14"), and the configs you have earned sit before the ones you have not. A chip carries the config's weight, its name, a tag naming the highest version it can climb to inside a run, and its headline figure as a badge. Hover or press the **i** for the effect in full and where the config came from: starter, or how you earned it. A config you have not earned is a dashed chip showing only its weight and a `???`; its **i** opens both ways of earning it with your progress on each, where the card used to print them in the open. The per-version prices and roll odds the cards listed are gone from the Dex; the shop's Upgrade panel still states them where you buy.
- **The run screens were rebuilt around the phone.** The press a screen is asking for is now always the rightmost thing in its bar, at every width, with the way back always on the left — below a certain width the forward press used to fall in beside the others and read as one of them. A screen offering a single press now gives it the whole row on a phone rather than a thumb-sized target in a corner. A note sitting under a press ("Or click ENTER") tucks in under it instead of floating a full row below. And the bar itself is pinned in the flow rather than floating over it, so it can no longer cover the end of a page it was only meant to close.
- **The coverage readout stays with you on a phone.** Scrolling to the answers used to take the meter with it, which left you picking without the one reading every choice is weighed against. The Coverage panel now pins to the top of the screen on a phone and lets go once the screen is wide enough to show everything at once.
- **The shop is named for the gate it is stocking you for.** It read "Shop · cleared Boulder", which named the gate behind you; it now reads **Cascade Shop**, which is the one you are buying for.
- **The gate debrief reads its balance the way every other screen does.** The heading led with a large `+61 KB` over a quiet "balance 357 KB", which put two numbers in one corner and made it unclear which was yours. It now states **Storage balance** over the balance itself, and the movement moved to where movement belongs: the payout table's last row now shows what you came in with, what you left with, and the step between them, each as a figure rather than as bare text.
- **Prep lists your subscriptions.** The audits heading has always said what the standing bill comes to on a clear; nothing said what made it up. A **Subscriptions** panel now lists every config charging rent and the build space itself, line by line, with the total under them — which is what you need to know which one to drop when the bill stops being affordable.
- **"free" says what it is pricing.** The Build panel's recurring badge read `↻ free` without ever naming what was free. Pressing it now explains that the build rents its space, that the rent falls due at every gate you clear, and that it stays at nothing until the build outgrows its rung.
- **Review answers moved to the bar.** It sat inside the answers panel, so finding it meant expanding a fold first. It is now beside the other exits at the bottom of the debrief, where every gate ends.
- **The coverage panel on a poll now reads in units against the gate's line.** Where it said `42% · HEALTHY` it now says `2.1 of 3 · HEALTHY`: the units you have banked over the run against the units this gate needs, with the HEALTHY mark under the bar stating the same figure. The percentage never fell because you lost something. It fell when you **cleared** a gate, because the next gate opens five more slots and divides the same score by a bigger number, and a number that drops after a win reads as a punishment. In units, clearing a gate leaves your figure exactly where it was and raises the line you are climbing to: 3 at Pallet, 6 at Boulder, 9 at Cascade. The bar still fills to the same proportion, and the sentence under it still says how many units across how many slots that is and what percentage it comes to. The debrief, the run-over screen, prep and the shop keep the percentage, since comparing one gate against another is what they are for.
- **Prep’s two audit panels now read as one exchange.** What rivals fired at you and who you can fire at sat side by side without either saying which way it pointed. Every incoming audit now names the player who sent it, with their face beside the row, and carries a **respond** press that jumps straight to their row on the panel below — or says why it cannot, when they are not one of the three rivals you were offered. Each rival you can aim at reads as a person too: their face, the gate your audit would land on, what their build costs to run, and the build itself. Opening one shows the audit that was rolled for them, what it does, and — when their build alone decides it — which of their configs it would take offline, lit up in the build above it.
- **You can only fire an audit from a gate that could be fired at in return.** Clearing Pallet strongly used to arm a shot you could take at a leader five gates ahead while nothing could reach you, because nothing lands before gate 3. Until gate 3 the screen now says so once, on a single **Audits** panel reading “Audits are unlocked at gate 3 · Thunder” — where two panels used to sit side by side stating the same lock in two different sentences. An audit armed early is not lost — it keeps until you get there.
- **The gate debrief says whether you earned the swatch.** The line under the title used to report that the bar filled and name the gate ahead, both of which the screen already shows. It now states the one thing only that window could win or lose: "You earned the Pallet Swatch!", or "You didn't earn the Pallet swatch".
- **A gate asks one thing and offers two, and all three read the same way.** Prep's objectives block is now three plain sentences, each line sitting tight under the statement it belongs to so the block reads as pairs rather than as four loose lines. The window's own answer track ("Pallet, five slots, 0 of 5") has moved up into the same panel, directly under the objectives and above the coverage bar, so what a gate asks and where you actually stand are read in one place. The required one is headed **Main objective** and reads "Finish at OK or better" with "to clear the gate" under it; the paragraph of arithmetic that followed it (what the band is in percent, how many of the five that costs, what stays shut if you miss) is gone, because the table directly below already prices every band. The extras lost their `+` and their indent and are stated identically: "Finish at PERFECT / to earn the Pallet swatch", and a new one, "Finish at HEALTHY or better / to arm an audit", which is the rule that arms your shot at a rival and until now was only ever mentioned inside the panel that holds it.
- **The band names are always badges.** HEALTHY, OK, PERFECT, SHAKY and DANGER now wear the ladder's own colours wherever a sentence names one, instead of sitting in the prose as shouted words.
- **The shot you fire at a rival is called an audit, not an attack.** The debrief chip reads **audit earned**, and prep's panel is **Your audit**. It was always an audit that landed on them; only the word you earned it by was different.
- **Answers are locked in from the poll itself.** The separate submit strip under the screen is gone. The press now sits in the poll's own footer beside the author's name, with the count it acts on next to it: "2 picked" and **Lock in 2 answers**. With nothing picked it is refused, and says what it wants: "pick an answer first" on a single-answer poll, "pick every answer that fits" on a select-all.
- **The press a screen is asking for is pinned to the foot of a phone.** Prep, the shop, a new run, the gate debrief, the review and the run-over screen all keep their bottom action in view while you scroll, the way the build footer already did on the poll screen. It settles back into the page at tablet width and above.
- **The scoring popover reads on a phone.** It is headed **Score info** now, its rows stack into a column rather than squeezing a case, its ladder and its note onto one line, and the "before the build multiplies it" aside is gone. The panel heading that lists what each poll paid is simply **Score**.
- **The new run screen says what it wants in one line.** The build note is now "Select configs up to 4 weight units", in place of the paragraph about renting space and being billed at every gate. The registry's "The hand costs no storage, only room" note is gone with it: the deal already prices itself in the panel head.
- **Panel headings drop their details onto a second line instead of breaking mid-phrase.** On a narrow screen "Poll 1 out of 5" was snapping in half to make room for the category and the option count beside it.
- **Screen footers stack on a phone.** Where a footer carries a note between two presses ("the shop stays open until Boulder starts"), the note was the only thing that could give, so on a narrow screen it collapsed into a column one word wide while the buttons kept their size. The three now stack, and only widen into a row when there is room for one.
- **The coverage reading under the bar is quieter.** "You have scored 0 units across 5 slots, which is 0.0% coverage" was set at body size, competing with the question underneath it; it now reads as the caption it is.
- **The scoring popover opens on a tap.** "What a poll pays" only ever opened on hover, which no phone has, so on a touch screen it could not be read at all. A press opens it now and a press anywhere else shuts it; hovering still works with a mouse.

- **The community board and the `/run` hub now wear the same skin as the rest of the game.** Both were the last screens still drawn in older kits, and they read as visitors from another build. The board's five polls used to hide behind a row of numbered tabs you pressed one at a time; now all five are on the page at once, each one a row you can open, with the most recent already open and the ones the seed has not dealt yet showing as sealed rather than missing. Every option still states its share, whether it was right, and whether it was yours, and now also how many people picked it and who they were. The `/run` hub keeps what it always said — the gate your climb is standing on, your swatch track, whether today's five are ready or how long until the next ones drop — and says it in the game's own chrome, with the wait sitting on the press that would have played so a spent day reads as a refusal rather than a missing button. Login, sign-up, the footer, the not-found page and the error screen came across the same way. Nothing about how any of it is scored has moved.
- **Build space stopped being a thing you shop for.** The shop used to sell you room by the rung — 4 free, then 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 — and bill you for the rung you held whether or not you had filled it. The idea was that headroom should cost something, so that widening was a real decision. In practice headroom bought you nothing at all: the only thing a wider rung is good for is installing a config, and you can buy the room at the moment you install it. So the right move was always the same one — hold the smallest rung that fits — and the panel's real job was making you do that arithmetic by hand every visit, and punishing you when you forgot. It is gone. **Your build now occupies the smallest rung it fits in, automatically, and pays that rung at every gate you clear.** The ladder and its prices have not moved a single KB. What moved is where the decision happens. Because the rungs are coarse, a 9-weight build still pays the 12 rung's 64 KB, and crossing from 4 weight to 5 costs 16 KB at every gate for the rest of the run — so **the threshold, not the draft price, is what a config really costs you**. That is why an install that crosses a rung now **asks twice**: the first press turns into a saffron **Confirm** and opens a note reading "Build space scales 4 → 6" and "Upkeep becomes 16 KB a gate", and the second press commits. An install that fits inside the room you already rent is a single press and says nothing. Selling narrows the rung again immediately, and still refunds half. Two smaller consequences: nothing is refused for **room** any more below 32, the top of the ladder, because a build that does not fit its rung simply rents the one above; and the Build panel now carries the bill in its own header, so what you hold, the room left before the next rung, and what you are paying all read on one line. A bill your balance cannot cover is still not fatal — the run pays for the widest rung it can afford and the shop will not let you leave until the build fits that space, which is the same lock the exit has always had.
- **The registry's version offer can jump now, and tells you the odds.** About one shop in eight the registry still rolls a newer version of something you have installed. It now starts one version up and flips a coin for every further version until the flip fails or the ladder ends, so a v1 is offered v2 half the time, v3 a quarter, v4 or v5 an eighth each. The row says which odds you hit ("1 in 4 rolls"), the price is the registry price whatever the version, and the coverage gate the shop's own Upgrade button enforces still does not apply. The Dex's config cards state each version's odds beside its price and the tab's footer explains the rule. Pressing that offer opens the config's version ladder, with the rung the roll landed on as the Buy press: it takes the offer at the registry price rather than sending you through the coverage-gated Upgrade path it used to.
- **A poll's author links to their GitHub.** The handle in the byline is the editor's GitHub username, so it is now a link — white and underlined, opening in a new tab so it never takes you out of a run. The words around it stay quiet.
- **Gates are measured in answers, and the bands grow with the climb.** Pallet now asks three of five right answers for HEALTHY and two for OK, and it still cannot end a run: miss the lot and you retry tomorrow. From Boulder on every gate has all five bands, and each is wide enough that one day's answers can always move you across one: OK sits one answer under the line at Boulder and three at the Champion, and the floor is wherever HEALTHY stood the day before, so a day you finished HEALTHY never opens the next one in DANGER, while a day you scraped through does. A gate also asks at least two right answers of the day itself. Coasting in on yesterday's coverage with one right answer holds the gate instead of clearing it, the debrief says "1 of 5 right, 2 needed" over a bar that keeps reading what you actually hold, and prep's clear line says so before you start. Older runs in the Dex are read against the new lines.
- **The answers are a framed list.** The options sit inside one bordered box with rules between them instead of floating as separate rows, so the question and its answers read as a single object rather than a stack of loose ones.
- **yarn.lock is now `.lock`.** The config that hands you the shop's padlock was named after one package manager's lockfile, and the thing it does has nothing to do with that package manager: Cargo, Composer, Bundler and Poetry all write the same artefact, and most of them spell it with the same extension. It is now **`.lock`**, which is also the name of the shop control it grants, so your build and the registry finally use the same word for it. Nothing about what it costs, what it pays, or how you unlock it has changed; only the name.
- **Cold Start was backwards, and now it runs the way its name does.** A cold start is the *slow* one — the first request that has to wake everything up before it can answer — and the config named after it was paying **double** on a gate's opening answer, which is the opposite of what every developer who has ever waited on one would expect. It now pays **nothing at all** for a gate's first answer and **×1.5** for every answer after it. Across a full five-poll window that averages exactly what it averaged before, so the config is no stronger and no weaker than it was; what changed is its shape. It used to be a smaller **Overclock**, front-loading the payout and asking you to open on your best category. It is now Overclock read backwards: a config that wants you to get the opener out of the way and then run. The two are worth holding together for the first time, because one cancels the other's opener and they compound on everything after. It stays a starter config, so a new account still opens with it, and its row on the poll screen now reads the dead opener as a loss rather than hiding it. A ceiling only bites at the top; a drain bites all the way along. Bundling the cap with your pipeline's width onto one row had a second cost: the plan was selling two things at once and you could not tell which one the bill was for. **In its place, two sources of room that do not overlap.** Every run opens on **4 spots** and reaches **8, 12, 16 and 24** by clearing gates 1, 4, 7 and 10 — the same free width at the same depth for every run, whatever it is holding or spending. **And a new shop section, Extra spots**, rents four steps on top of that: **+1, +2, +3, +4**, at a flat **8 KB a gate per spot**, so +3 spots is simply three times +1 with nothing to work out. It is a picker rather than a row of presses — one choice, and dropping back to **none** is how you cancel rent you can no longer carry. Every row states the width it makes ("+2 spots · makes 10"), because that is the figure the decision is actually about. **Which steps are for sale depends on how deep you are**: +2 opens when you clear gate 2, +3 at gate 5, +4 at gate 8, and the steps you cannot rent yet still sit there saying which clear will open them. That staging is what stops a lucky early balance buying the summit pipeline at gate 1 — if money bought room, and room bought score, and score bought money, a lucky run would spiral. **Rent is a subscription, not a purchase.** It is charged at every clear for the rest of the run, and the free width the gates hand over later stacks on top of what you rent rather than paying it off. There is no way to buy a step outright: the section has exactly one control on it. **Fall behind on the rent and the step goes back**, dropping you to the width the gates gave you. That is the only thing in the game that makes a pipeline narrower, and it can leave a build sitting over its own capacity — the shop will not let you climb until you minify, uninstall, or rent the room back. The widest a pipeline ever gets is **28 spots**: the gates' 24, plus four. A git tag rescue opens on the width its depth owes and rents nothing, since width is a function of how deep the run is and a rescue starts deep. One consequence worth knowing: the **Subscriptions** section on the gate receipt now lists your spot rent beside your subscribed configs (Freemium), and both bill on a clear only — so a redo still costs nothing recurring.
- **The shop says less and shows more**: the two lists that are the shop's whole reason for existing — the shelf and your pipeline — no longer fold away, and neither does the capacity ladder; the carets are gone from all three. The amber line under the track that spelled out how to make room ("IndexedDB needs 2 spots · uninstall something") is gone too: an offer you have no room for already says so on its own badge, and the track's own caption already says how much room is left. Pipeline rows dropped the green **worth N KB** chip, since the Uninstall press beside it quotes the same refund. What is left on a row is what the config pays you. The shelf lost two more columns in the same pass: the **needs a bit** line in the middle of each row, and the grade word at the end of it. Both were the glyph's job — the squares in front of a name *are* the grade, and hovering them gives the size in spots ("takes 4 of 14 spots") — while a config you have no room for already greys its price and says what it needs when you reach for it. The empty rings in front of every offer went too: locking an offer for the next shop is not on sale in the first shops, and a ring with nothing in it read as a checkbox on a list you buy from rather than tick. Once locking *is* on sale it shows as a padlock, dimmed while you cannot afford it. On the pipeline track beside it, the two kinds of nothing finally look different: a spot standing **open** is dashed, as before, while room above the rung you hold is **hatched** with diagonal stripes. It is a distinction worth a glance, because one of them takes a config right now and the other takes a gate clear or a payment first. The hatching stays unlabelled, and hovering it names how the room arrives. And a **byte** on that track now wears the prismatic ring — the same shifting Kanto edge the Upgrade press wears — instead of a plain gold border. It is the grade that used to be called *legendary*, and eight spots of your pipeline deserves to look like it cost that. Minify it and the ring goes: the dotted edge that says "this one is halved" is the more useful of the two, and the ring would paint straight over it.
- **Open a config anywhere and it tells you the same three things**: unfold a config — in the opening deal, on the shop shelf, in your pipeline on prep, or on the rail while you answer — and under its name you get what it does in a sentence, then one muted line of facts: how far it is **levelled**, what it **pays**, and what it would **sell for**. Every surface reads from one line now, so the same config cannot describe itself differently in two places, and the detail sits behind a rule tying it to its own row instead of running into the row below. The grade is deliberately not on that line: the row's own mark states it, and saying it twice was the legend problem one row further down. Two details worth knowing: a config that has never been upgraded still says "level 1", while one with no upgrade path at all says nothing about levels (there is no level 2 to reach), and the refund quoted is what **this build** would actually be paid — Freemium's discount and WTFPL's no-warranty clause both move it, so a shelf that quoted list price would be quoting a number the Uninstall press refuses to honour. In the deal there is no refund line at all, because nothing has been bought yet.
- **Rarity is a shape now, not a colour — and the tiers have real names**: `common / uncommon / rare / legendary` are gone. A config's grade is a **bit**, a **crumb**, a **nibble** or a **byte** — 1, 2, 4 and 8 bits — and it is drawn as exactly that many little squares in front of the name. The mark *is* the definition: a nibble is four bits, so the nibble glyph is four cells, and more cells means rarer with nothing to memorise. The old rarity colours are gone with the old names — a rare config's red bar used to sit in the same shop row as the red **Uninstall** press and mean something unrelated — and the four grades wear a plain ramp instead: **grey, blue, purple, gold**, bit up to byte, on the squares and on the grade's name at the end of the row. Red and green stay out of it, because in this game those two mean *refused* and *recommended* wherever else you meet them. The four-colour key under the deal and the shop shelf is gone all the same: a ladder that runs grey to gold has nothing left to key. Hover the squares on any config and they tell you the price in plain numbers — "takes 4 of 14 spots" — and the Dex's Configs tab still spells the whole ladder out, glyph, name, odds and size together. A config's **level** got its own separate mark at the same time, a small segmented bar beside the upgrade button, and unlike the grade it does show its empty segments — a level is a distance along a ladder you can still buy more of, so the room left is part of the information.
- **A config's grade is now what it costs your pipeline**: your pipeline holds **spots**, and a config takes as many as its grade has bits — a **bit** takes 1, a **crumb** 2, a **nibble** 4, a **byte** 8. The little squares in front of its name *are* the price: count the cells and you know the room. Every screen that shows your build now draws it as that room — a bar per config, as wide as the spots it takes, one dashed stretch for whatever is left, and a line underneath reading "10 spots free · a byte fits". A config too big for the room left dims where it sits, so you find out before you tick it rather than after. That makes the grade ladder mean something for the first time; a byte used to cost the same one slot as a bit, so being rare bought you nothing but a bigger number. Now a byte is a genuine dilemma wherever you meet it — early it literally cannot fit, and late it costs you most of your build. **KB is untouched**: it still buys offers, upgrades, rebuilds, locks, extensions and the git tag, at the same prices as before, and it never buys width. Spots come from clearing gates and, past a byte, from renting them. Keeping the two apart is the whole point: if money bought room, and room bought score, and score bought money, a lucky run would spiral into twenty configs and an unreadable score reveal. **Minify** is the new pressure valve — halve a config's spots and halve what it gives, one way only. It is how you squeeze a byte into a four-spot pipeline, and how you pay a peel without losing anything. A bit cannot be minified: one spot is the floor.
- **A config's figure reads differently depending on what the screen is for**: in a list you are choosing from — the opening deal, your pipeline on prep — every rate now sits as plain text in one column down the right, so nothing competes for attention and an odd one out (a `+0.5` among a column of `×1.25`) is visible at a glance instead of being one more framed badge. Beside a poll the chips stay, because there they carry the will-pay / did-pay grammar.
- **Every answer now settles before the run moves on**: submitting used to jump straight to the next poll, so what an answer paid — and which configs paid it — flashed by unseen. Now the answered poll stays put, and the score reads out twice, once as math and once as machinery. Under the options, the earn shows as the multiplication it actually is, with every contributor named — **correct 1.0 × streak 1.2 × .ts 1.25 × AGENTS.md 2**, each config's chip keyed by its rarity stripe. A config that adds a flat amount instead of multiplying joins the row with a **+**, quoting what it actually paid (`Code Coverage +0.5` — the same figure its rail row badges), and the base plus its adds are bracketed so it reads the way it is counted: the multipliers scale the sum, not the base alone. Underneath comes "this answer paid **+2.9%**", because those chips work out to the number and you can check them. Next to the **Next poll →** button, one line keeps the stakes in view: "2 to go · 0.1% short of clearing Pallet" (or that the gate's demand is already met); after the window's fifth answer it goes quiet, because the gate has decided and the click should reveal it, not spoil it. On the rail, the chips got a grammar: an outlined gray chip is what a config **would** pay (`×1.25`, `+0.5`), a filled one is what it **did** — every config that contributed trades its rate for a green `paid +0.5` badge the moment the answer lands, losses wear the same badge in red, and configs that pay storage badge their KB the same way (IndexedDB's `paid +8 KB`, clamp included). The header follows: while you answer it promises ("2 will apply"), after you answer it reports ("2 applied · 1 skipped"), and an online config that contributed nothing owns up with "unused". Your picks sit badged against the key — "expected" in green, "you picked" in green or red depending on whether you should have — with the also-rans stepped back. A wrong answer keeps the rail silent on purpose: your configs never touch losses, so the loss reads once, on the paid line, and nothing pretends the build failed when the answer did. The poll's explanation lands here too, at the moment you actually want to know why. On timed gates the next poll's clock waits until you click **Next** — reading your result never bills the answer you haven't seen.
- **Early gates now warn you that audits are coming**: the first three gates run without audits, and their receipts used to drop the Audit section entirely — so the first audit you ever met was gate 3 charging double fees. A clean gate's receipt now reads "None scheduled" and names the first audit waiting ahead, so you learn to read the fine print while it is still free.
- **The run-start screen tells you where your streak stops paying**: the gate panel now lists a **streak cap** under the coverage demand and the audit count. Answering correctly in a row has always paid 10% more per answer up to double, and the ceiling was never stated anywhere. It reads as a property of your build rather than a rule of the gate, because configs that sell you more headroom are coming, and this is the number they will move.
- **A missed gate peels a share of your pipeline, not a count of configs**: 20% early rising to 35% at the summit, paid however you like. Peeling "one config" used to take a quarter of an opening build or a whole byte off a summit build for the same word. And before gate 3 the quota can never exceed half your build, which minifying alone always covers — so a one-config opening survives its first miss instead of dying to it.
- **The pipeline tells you which of your configs is actually doing something**: while you answer, every row on the pipeline rail now says where that config stands on the poll in front of you. A filled green dot means **online** — it is working on this question. A hollow dot means **skipped**, and the row says what it was waiting for: ESLint reads "js and ts only" on a CSS poll, Freemium reads "bills at the gate clear", Cold Start reads "the gate's first poll only". A red dot means **offline** — an audit has switched it off — with the audit named and the config's own name struck through. The fold's header sums it as a promise ("2 will apply", plus an offline count when an audit is holding something), so a build that looks full but is half asleep on this category reads as one line instead of six. A skipped config also drops its number, because a multiplier sitting beside a row that is doing nothing reads as coverage you are earning. Until now every row wore a green tick or a red cross, which came from an older version of the game where each config carried a check it could pass or fail; configs have not been graded like that for a while, and the rail was still saying they were. The shop and the prep page list your configs with no status at all, since online, skipped and offline are all facts about a specific poll, and neither screen has one yet.
- **The Earth gate's audit is now called Breaking Change**: same rule as before — your most-upgraded config is switched off for the whole attempt — but the name "Deprecated" now belongs to the config above, whose mechanic actually *is* deprecation (works, fades, gets removed). The audit was always a breakage, and it hits the config at the highest version, which is exactly where breaking changes land.
- **Every upgrade costs storage now, and the git tag is priced by the gate it marks**: upgrading a Focus config used to be free — reach 5% JavaScript coverage and the level was yours — which left an Upgrade button in the shop with no price on it at all. It has one now, the same 32KB × level every other upgrade pays. Coverage still decides whether you have *earned* the level; storage decides whether you can *afford* it, and neither covers for the other, so the tooltip names whichever one is standing in your way. Your mastery of a category is no longer a free win: cashing it in competes with drafting for the same KB. The **git tag** stopped costing a flat 512KB, which made a shallow checkpoint a bad deal and a deep one a steal: it now costs 128KB at gate 4 and 64KB more per gate after that, up to 512KB at gate 10 — and gate 10 is the last one that sells you one, because a rescue any deeper drops a three-config starter build into stacked audits and a four-config peel, which is a fortune spent on a death. It is still one rescue per purchase: the run it saves spends it, and the shop will sell you another.
- **Every gate from Thunder now audits you differently, and the deeper you go the more of them stack**: an audit is a rule the gate itself carries, printed on the Build Summary before you walk in. The first three gates stay clean while you learn the loop; from gate 3 every gate carries one, from Seafoam two, and Elite and Champion three at once. **Cost Overrun** doubles what the linter and the community peek charge. Four audits knock your own configs out, and they differ in how they pick: **Dependency Outage** takes one offline for the whole attempt, **Flaky Build** rolls a fresh one on every poll (it can hit the same config twice), **Rolling Outage** steps through your pipeline so a different config is down each poll, and **Deprecated** switches off whichever config you upgraded most — a tax on having a favourite, picking at random among them if you have levelled nothing. Whatever is down is named on the answering screen and marked on the pipeline itself — the row reads `offline`, its name struck through, the audit that did it named beside it, and any paid action it sold disappears, because a switched-off config cannot deliver what you would be paying for. It keeps its slot the whole time: the build has not changed, the effect just does nothing. Reloading never re-rolls which one it is, and the shop before the gate says nothing about it — that would be telling you the roll before you got there. **Read-only** shuts the shop standing before the gate: nothing bought, sold, upgraded or switched, so you climb it with exactly the build you already had (and if you miss, the retry's shop is shut too). **Feature Freeze** goes further and takes the paid actions away entirely. **Timeout** puts the window's first polls on a clock — 30 seconds at Seafoam, 25 at Earth, 20 at the Champion — with the countdown sitting beside the audit's warning; nothing auto-submits, but an answer that lands late counts as a miss whatever you picked, and the review still shows you were right. **Memory Leak** is the Volcano's storage drain, renamed and softened to −16KB a poll and −32KB on a miss. The **Mirror** is the big one, and it works differently than it first did: rather than paying you for wrong answers, it flips the question. Every poll at a mirrored gate asks for the **incorrect** options — and wants all of them, so a single-answer poll with four options becomes a three-option select-all, which is the same knowledge and a great deal more precision. Everything else about it is normal: streaks build, partials pay, the reveal marks what the gate asked for, and Marsh charges its full price instead of the discount the old mirror needed. It returns at Elite alongside the strip and a flaky build. And because naming every wrong option proves exactly the knowledge that naming the right one does, a mirrored answer counts as **right** on the community board — mixed in with everyone else's. The one place it cannot be mixed is the community split you pay Telemetry for: that sells what the room thinks the answer is, so answers given under the mirror are left out of it entirely rather than quietly inverting the percentages you bought. Volkswagen CI still reports the gate's *first* audit as passing, which now matters much more — at a three-audit gate you are choosing which rule to erase.
- **The gate is a CI run: it reads your coverage report now, and failing it sends you shopping**: every gate demands a total-coverage threshold alongside your build's checks — 3% at the teaching gate, 12% at Boulder, up to 365% at the summit, each priced at 80% of a perfect run's base pace so streaks and multipliers stay headroom rather than homework. The Build Summary is rebuilt around the demand and now reads as three blocks. **Clear the gate** lists the two things a clear actually takes — answer all 5 polls, and reach 12% total coverage, the second riding a thin rail on the same line ("9% / 12%", red until you're over the target, green once you're past it) — then what a miss costs ("remove 1 config, then retry this gate"). The poll count used to sit in the header as a caption; it's a requirement, so it's listed as one. **Rewards** reads the same way, one list item per payout: what a correct answer pays, what the clear pays — with the per-answer coverage now saying plainly when its Focus bonus applies ("×1.25 on a matching poll", not just "with a matching config") — and the swatch the gate is holding for you, drawn as the dashed, colourless chip your collection uses for a badge you haven't earned. **Game over** states the one rule that ends a run, in the terms the run actually dies by: "your run ends if your pipeline holds fewer than 2 configs — you hold 3", turning red and naming the peel when you're already under it. The receipt's **Rewards** half follows you past the gate too: the clear screen previews just the *next* gate's payout under **Next up** — what a correct answer and a clear are worth there, and the swatch it's holding for you — a clear is a payout, not a threat, so the demand and the game-over rule stay off it. The failed-gate repair screen carries the redo's whole receipt under **Retry**, where the config floor updates live as you peel — the screen where you choose what to sacrifice is finally the screen that tells you what the choice costs. The run-over screen closes the loop with the same sentence in the past tense ("Your pipeline held 2 configs — gate 4 ends a run below 4"), instead of the old catch-all about being stripped bare. And the whole ladder is now readable from gate 0: tap any pip on the HUD's gate bar and it names what that gate will ask for ("Needs 60% total coverage · 4 configs"), so the summit's price is something you can look up on day one rather than discover on arrival. Gates you've already cleared just show the badge — the demand is history there. Come up short at the window's close and the gate fails exactly like a failed check: the strip lands, and then — this is new — **the shop opens before the replay**. Every redo now routes strip → shop → prep, so the storage you banked is the comeback resource: rebuy what the strip took, then start the same gate again on fresh polls. Camping a gate is priced without any new rule, because the storage bill still collects on every closed window, pass or fail, and only the attempt that passes pays the gate reward. Death hasn't moved: strips that sink the build under the gate's width demand still end the run at the door, and the door now grades every attempt — the old carve-out that let a stripped-thin build replay unexamined is gone, since a shop finally sits where it needed one. To keep the redo survivable the wrong-answer bleed softens from half the base gain to a quarter, which moves break-even accuracy from one-in-three to one-in-five: above that, every window nets progress toward the threshold, so a struggling run grinds forward while the attempts cost it. And because coverage runs past 100% now, the HUD reads it in laps named after real rungs of testing rigor — Line to 100%, then "Branch 70%" for 170%, Mutation past 200%, Fuzz past 300%.
- **Gates grant slots — the coverage ladder is retired**: your pipeline still grows from 3 slots to 14, but width now arrives with the climb itself: clearing gate 1 opens slot 4 and every clear after it opens the next, topping out at gate 11. There is nothing to reach for and nothing to buy — the dashed row in the pipeline list now just says "Opens when Gate 2 clears", and the green "Unlocked Nth slot" acknowledgment appears in the shop as before. The old ladder ("Reach 45% total coverage to widen") is gone because the gate itself demands your coverage now, and paying the same score to two different ladders was one ladder too many. Runs already wider than their gate would grant keep every slot they earned.
- **Every config now asks you for something, and the linters ask for the right thing**: a build used to be able to owe the gate nothing at all. ESLint and Stylelint only bound if you paid to use them, and AGENTS.md carried no demand whatsoever, so a pipeline of those three had an empty checklist and cleared every gate without answering a single poll correctly, all the way to the summit with all 13 swatches. Two things change. **The linters now ask for competence instead of proof of purchase**: ESLint wants one JavaScript or TypeScript poll right if either turns up, Stylelint wants one CSS poll right if CSS turns up. Exactly like a Focus config, and excused only by a draw that never showed you the category. Using the effect stays entirely optional, because a demand you can't afford is a death you never chose: lint a poll and get it wrong and you're out the fee, nothing more. **AGENTS.md now asks for one correct answer**, unconditionally, never excused by the draw. A legendary's 256KB price is most of what it costs, so its demand is light, but light isn't free. One honest gap remains: if none of your configs' categories are drawn all window, every demand is excused and the gate asks nothing. That's the rule working as intended, and you can't plan around a draw you don't choose.
- **Unit Tests asks for exactly what you bought**: its correct-answer demand no longer creeps up as you climb. An un-upgraded Unit Tests wants 1 correct answer in the window at gate 0 and still wants 1 at the summit; buying L2 makes it 2, L3 makes it 3, and that is the only thing that ever moves it. Previously the demand rose by one every second gate on its own (up to +3), which meant the same config on the same screen quietly asked for more each visit — and since a level is what raises a demand everywhere else in the game, it read as though the config had upgraded itself. It never had. What still gets harder as you climb is the price of failing: a lost gate strips more configs the deeper you are, and the gates keep demanding a wider pipeline. The difficulty now comes from what a fail costs you, not from a number changing behind your back.
- **Buying in the shop now takes two taps, and the first one only opens the card**: hovering an offer just whispers "Click to install"; clicking it opens the offer's card — its rarity, what it does, a green **install** button and, once holds unlock, a **Lock config** button — and a click anywhere else closes it again. Installing settles the offer into **✓ owned**, staying on the table so you can see what you took. Selecting costs nothing and you can change your mind as often as you like, so the only press that spends storage is a button inside a card you deliberately opened. Previously the chip itself bought the config the instant you touched it, which on a phone meant a mistimed thumb could spend 384KB with no way back. Offers you can't afford (or have no free slot for) stay tappable so you can still read them; press the greyed-out **install** and it tells you what's in the way ("No free slot — uninstall a config first", "Costs 128KB — you have 8KB").
- **The Build Summary shows what a correct answer is worth**: a new "Per answer" line reports the coverage and storage a single correct answer pays right now, before you've answered a poll — and calls out the extra coverage a Focus config adds when its category actually comes up ("×1.25 with a matching config"). Hovering a bench config or shop offer updates the line the same way the clear reward already does, so you can see exactly what adding it would change. The clear reward's own coverage figure now reads "×2 coverage this gate" instead of a bare "×2", so it can't be mistaken for a KB or reward multiplier.
- **After the shop, the prep page is home base**: leaving the shop now lands you on the gate-prep page instead of pushing you to the community board, and the shop stays open behind it — bounce shop ↔ prep ↔ community as long as you like, because the climb only resumes when you press **Start gate** on prep. That's also where leftover storage above your cap finally burns, so a detour to the community board no longer costs you your overflow. When today's polls are spent, the start button itself wears the countdown ("New polls in 7h 23m") while the shop and community stay a click away — so the wait is now time you spend tuning your build. The stake's "To start" line carries its own little **← Back to shop** shortcut, so the demand it names and the place you meet it sit on the same row.
- **Deep gates demand a real build**: each gate now admits only a pipeline wide enough to survive its own stake — nothing at the first gate, then 1, 2, 3 configs at Boulder, Cascade and Thunder, climbing to 8 at the summit — so you can no longer sell down to one easy config and cruise past every check. The early gates stay light on purpose: farm them, rebuild there after a rough strip. The shop and gate-prep screens refuse to sell or drop below the coming gate's demand, the Build Summary names it in red with the exact count to install once you're under it, and the shop's exit simply stays locked until you've repaired the width — a strip can leave you under the demand for the retry, but you shop your way back over it before the next gate, never into a surprise death. If the run truly can't afford the repair, the exit turns red and says so — **End run** is then your click to make, not the gate's. Trimming your pipeline mid-window is gone too: the gate judges the build it let in.
- **Start your run with one pick**: run setup now offers three starter stacks, each its own card — **React** (.js/.jsx, "Fast but risky"), **TypeScript** (.js/.ts/ESLint, "Safer JS/TS focus," marked **Recommended** for your first run) and **Full stack** (.vue/.java/.git, "Balanced across categories") — named for what's actually inside and tuned so none of them is a hidden hard mode. Picking one expands its card into a compact preview — every config's demand and payoff on one line each — with a linter's escalating fee (and anything else beyond the headline mechanic) waiting behind its own "more details" tap, so a three-config stack doesn't read like a rules document. Prefer full control? A dashed **Customize all 3 slots →** card opens the classic bench ("← Back to stacks" brings you back) — free drafting from the whole roster, not just the curated three. The Build Summary beside it now reads as one flowing sequence — polls → clear reward → fail consequence — instead of separate boxed sections, and drops "0/1" counters before you've actually played a poll.
- **Clearing a gate now pays out instead of reporting**: the gate-clear screen is one big number — "+64KB storage" — with the gate's name above it, one line telling you what storage is for, and a **Spend it → the shop** button that takes you straight to where you can. The per-config breakdown, meters and climb ladder are gone from the clear; the failed-gate screen keeps its full report, because that's where you need to know what fell short. Your storage bill (and any downgrade) still shows up here — that's news, not noise.
- **Storage capacity is now a subscription**: every run starts on the free 512 KB plan, and the shop offers bigger ones — 640 KB for 8 KB a gate, or 768 KB for 16. The bill lands every time a window closes, win or lose: a failed gate pays you nothing and still charges you. Can't cover the bill? You drop straight back to the free plan, the way any cloud provider would handle it. Downgrading yourself is free but immediate — anything sitting above the smaller cap burns on the spot, and the button warns you exactly how much before you press it. The gate-prep screen lists the bill next to the gate's stake ("pass or fail"), and the reward and repair screens show the receipt.
- **A fail you cannot pay for ends the run**: a failed gate peels configs, and from around gate 4 the peel wants more configs than a lean pipeline holds. That now ends the run right there, instead of emptying your pipeline and sending you into five more polls you had no way of clearing. The pipeline header has been warning you all along ("a fail peels all 3, run over"); now it is the truth. Your last config also stopped being for sale in the shop, so a run only ever ends at a gate it failed.
- **The answer review reads like a failing test**: open a question on "Review your answers" and you now get **Expected** over **Received**, each option tagged with the letter it wore in the question (A, B, C…), so the gap between the right answer and yours is one glance instead of a hunt down a list. The right answer reads green; yours reads red when it was wrong and amber when it was only half right, which means the two rows share a colour only when you nailed it. Multi-answer polls close with what you caught and the letters you didn't ("1 of 2, you missed C"). Every option neither side touched folds away behind "7 other options", so a nine-option question no longer fills the screen with things nobody chose. Questions you got right arrive folded and dimmed while the ones you fumbled arrive open, since those are the reason you came, and the PASS/PART/FAIL badges are outlined here so a column of them stays quieter than the answers underneath.
- **The gate's payout reads as three meters**: storage, coverage and the climb now report the same way — where the run stands ("96KB storage"), what this gate added ("+64KB this gate"), a bar, and what the bar is filling toward. Storage fills toward the 512KB cap. Coverage's meter is the slot it is buying — the same dashed unlock row the shop draws, with the rung's price, how far you've come, and a **locked** / **unlocked** pill — so the reward screen answers "did that open a slot" without a trip to the shop. When a gate's coverage is what carried you over the rung, a green line under it says so: "slot 4 opened this gate". The badge line counts the collection ("earned · 2 of 13") and sits over the gate ladder, so the pip you just filled is visible and the caption names where you're going ("gate 2 of 12 · next up: Cascade gate").
